East Africa

Discover Kenya

From the snow-capped peaks of Mount Kenya to the golden plains of the Maasai Mara, Kenya is a land of breathtaking diversity in landscape,people, food, and culture. Come explore.

Top Travel Destinations

Kenya offers some of the most iconic wildlife and natural wonders on the African continent. Here are three must-visit destinations.

Wildlife

Maasai Mara National Reserve

Home to the world-famous Great Migration, the Maasai Mara is one of the most spectacular wildlife reserves on Earth. Every year, over 1.5 million wildebeest thunder across the Mara River. The reserve teems with lions, elephants, cheetahs, and hundreds of bird species, making it a paradise for safari lovers and photographers alike.

Beach

Diani Beach

Stretching for over 17 kilometres along Kenya's Indian Ocean coastline, Diani Beach is consistently ranked among the best beaches in Africa. Its powdery white sand, turquoise waters, and coral reefs make it a top destination for snorkelling, diving, and kite-surfing.The surrounding forests are also home to Angola colobus monkeys.

Adventure

Mount Kenya National Park

Standing at 5,199 metres, Mount Kenya is Africa's second-highest peak and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The mountain offers trekking routes for both novice hikers and experienced mountaineers. Stunning equatorial glaciers and spectacular views reward those who make the climb, and the surrounding park is rich in wildlife.

Famous Kenyan Foods

Kenyan cuisine is hearty, flavourful, and deeply tied to community.These three dishes are loved across the country and are a must-try for any visitor.

Grilled

Nyama Choma

Nyama Choma - literally "grilled meat" in Swahili - is Kenya's unofficial national dish. Goat or beef is slow-roasted over an open charcoal fire and served with kachumbari (a fresh tomato and onion salad) and ugali. It is the centrepiece of celebrations, weekend gatherings, and sports viewings across the country.

Staple

Ugali

Ugali is the staple food of Kenya, eaten by people of virtuallyn every ethnic group. Made by cooking white maize flour in boiling water until it forms a firm dough, ugali is shaped into balls and used to scoop up stews, greens (sukuma wiki), or meat. Simple yet deeply satisfying, it is eaten at breakfast, lunch and dinner every day.

Spiced Rice

Pilau

Pilau is a fragrant spiced rice dish that reflects Kenya's centuries-old ties with Arab and Indian traders along the Swahili Coast. Cooked with cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, and cumin, and layered with tender beef or chicken, pilau is a dish reserved for celebrations, weddings, and Friday lunches. Its rich aroma draws a crowd from streets away.

Kenyan Cultures and Traditions

Kenya is home to over 40 ethnic groups, each with its own language,traditions, and art. Here are three of the most celebrated cultural expressions in the country.

Ethnic Group

Maasai Culture

The Maasai are one of Kenya's most iconic ethnic groups, renowned for their distinctive red shukas (blankets), elaborate beaded jewellery, and the adumu - a competitive jumping dance performed by young warriors. Their villages (manyattas) remain a living window into a rich and ancient way of life that has endured for centuries.

Music & Dance

Luo Music & Dance

The Luo community from western Kenya around Lake Victoria have a deep musical heritage. Their traditional instrument, the nyatiti - an eight-stringed lyre — is played alongside energetic rhythms and call-and-response vocals. Luo music gave rise to benga, a genre blending traditional rhythms with electric guitar that has influenced musicians across East Africa.

Tradition

Kikuyu Rites of Passage

The Kikuyu are Kenya's largest ethnic group and have a rich tradition of rites of passage marking key life transitions. Initiation ceremonies are community events celebrated with songs, feasts, and storytelling that reinforce values of courage and belonging. The spirit of harambee (pulling together),championed by Kenya's first president Jomo Kenyatta, stems from this deep tradition of communal support.
