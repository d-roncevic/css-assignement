Southeastern Balkans

Discover Albania

Nestled in the heart of the Western Balkans, Albania is a country of rugged mountains, turquoise coastlines, and ancient traditions — still largely undiscovered, and all the more magical for it.

Places to Visit

From UNESCO-listed old towns to pristine Ionian beaches, Albania packs extraordinary variety into a small geography.

UNESCO

Berat

Known as the "City of a Thousand Windows," Berat's Ottoman-era houses stacked up a hillside below a Byzantine castle make it one of the best-preserved historic towns in the Balkans.

Beach

Albanian Riviera

Crystal-clear Ionian waters, hidden coves, and laid-back fishing villages stretch along Albania's southern coast — often compared to the Greek islands but without the crowds or the prices.

UNESCO

Gjirokastër

A dramatic stone city draped across a mountain ridge, Gjirokastër is home to a formidable Ottoman bazaar, a looming 12th-century castle, and the birthplace of writer Ismail Kadare.

Albanian Cuisine

Albanian food is honest, seasonal, and deeply rooted in the land — think slow-baked lamb, hand-rolled pastries, and dairy straight from highland farms.

National Dish

Tavë Kosi

Albania's most iconic dish: tender lamb baked low and slow under a rich blanket of egg-and-yoghurt custard. Simple ingredients, extraordinary depth of flavour. Every Albanian grandmother has her own version, and every version is the best one.

Street Food

Byrek

Flaky filo pastry filled with salty white cheese, spinach, or minced meat — baked in large trays and sold by the slice at bakeries across every Albanian town.

Traditional

Fërgesë

A comforting skillet of roasted peppers, tomatoes, and cottage cheese — a staple of Tirana's home kitchens and a beloved vegetarian classic.

Culture & Traditions

Albania's culture is a living mosaic — shaped by Illyrian roots, Ottoman centuries, and a fierce, proud independence that has defined the Albanian spirit for millennia.

UNESCO

Iso-Polyphony

A UNESCO Intangible Heritage — haunting, layered multi-voice singing unlike anything else in European folk music.

Code of Honor

Besa

"To keep the promise." During WWII, Albanian families sheltered Jewish refugees under this ancient code of honour at great personal risk.

Folklore

Valle

Albania's traditional circle dance, performed at weddings and festivals. Each region has its own style, costume, and rhythm.
